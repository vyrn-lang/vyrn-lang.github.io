std/narrow

30 exports, from std/narrow.vyrn.

Narrow number formats for storage: Float16, BFloat16, the two FP8 formats, the OCP MX blocks, the NVFP4 block and packed Int4. A program widens a value to Float32, computes, and narrows the result; the narrow types have no arithmetic of their own.

Every conversion is integer code over the Float32 bit view, so every engine gives the same bits. toFloat32 is exact. A narrowing rounds half to even and gives infinity past the largest finite value where the format has one, else NaN; its Sat form gives the largest finite value. A NaN narrows to the format's NaN code with the sign cleared. BFloat16 is Float32 rounded to its upper 16 bits, subnormals kept.

toNvFp4 fn

std/narrow.vyrn, line 454
fn toNvFp4(xs: read Array<Float32, 16>) -> NvFp4Block

The NVFP4 block of xs. The scale is the largest magnitude divided by 6, rounded to UE4M3 and saturated; each element is its value divided by the scale, rounded to E2M1 and saturated. An infinity counts as the largest finite Float32, and a NaN sets the scale to 127 and every element to 0.

Test names
  • the converter gives the hashes of its exhaustively checked C twin
  • every finite code widens to the value that narrows back to it
  • Float16 widens as F16C does and BFloat16 as a shift
  • the scalar formats round, overflow and keep a NaN's class
  • an MX block shares the scale of its largest magnitude
  • an NVFP4 block scales by its largest magnitude over 6
  • Int4 values pack two to a byte and wrap
