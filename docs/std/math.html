std/math

16 exports, from std/math.vyrn.

Integer helpers (min, max, abs, clamp) and Float64 functions (pi, rounding, sine, cosine, exp, log, pow, tan, atan2), written in Vyrn. The rounding functions are exact, and exp, log, pow, tan and atan2 state their error in ulps against the exact result.

sin fn

std/math.vyrn, line 99
fn sin(x: Float64) -> Float64

Sine of x radians.

Reduces x to [-pi/2, pi/2] with sin(x) = sin(pi - x) and a period of 2pi, then evaluates the odd Taylor polynomial through x^13 in Horner form; the error stays under 1e-13. Every step is one rounded operation and native code compiles with -ffp-contract=off, so every engine returns the same bits.

log fn

The natural logarithm of x. Within 1 ulp. Zero gives -infinity, a negative x or NaN gives NaN, and infinity gives infinity.

pow fn

std/math.vyrn, line 225
fn pow(x: Float64, y: Float64) -> Float64

x raised to y. Within 1 ulp, and exact where the result is a whole number a Float64 holds. pow(x, 0.0) and pow(1.0, y) are 1 even for NaN; a negative x to a fractional y is NaN; otherwise the C99 rules for zeros and infinities hold.

tan fn

atan2 fn

std/math.vyrn, line 448
fn atan2(y: Float64, x: Float64) -> Float64

The angle of the point (x, y) from the positive x axis, in radians within [-pi, pi]. Within 2 ulps. The signs of zeros and infinities pick the quadrant as C99 states: atan2(0.0, -1.0) is pi and atan2(-0.0, -1.0) is -pi. NaN in either gives NaN.

Tests

Test names
  • sine hits the values the unit circle fixes
  • sine stays in range far from the origin
  • abs saturates at the minimum instead of wrapping negative
  • the rounding functions are exact and keep the sign of a zero
  • exp is within 1 ulp and saturates at its edges
  • log is within 1 ulp, subnormals included
  • pow is within 1 ulp and exact on whole results
  • tan is within 1 ulp at any finite argument
  • atan2 is within 2 ulps and takes the quadrant from the signs
